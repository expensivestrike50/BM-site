Masonry Development FAQs

Answers about masonry products, feedstock choices, formulations and physical testing. Consider durability.

What is development for masonry products?

Material development connects feedstock characteristics with product requirements, manufacturing equipment and processing conditions. For blocks, pavers and masonry, it involves comparing possible formulations, selecting practical experiments and measuring performance. Computational recommendations can inform these decisions, but physical testing remains essential before manufacturing or application.

Which feedstocks can be explored?

Potential inputs include recycled fines, mineral powders and alternative aggregates. Suitability depends on chemical composition, physical properties, consistency and the intended application. Available measurements help guide screening, while experiments assess strength, durability and process compatibility. A waste stream is not automatically a suitable construction ingredient; safety, environmental requirements and material variability must be assessed.

What does Better Materials recommend?

Better Materials is developing a computational R&D platform to recommend formulations, manufacturing conditions and targeted validation experiments. For masonry products, the goal is to consider strength, appearance and dry-cast processing alongside equipment constraints, cost and carbon objectives. These are development recommendations, not product certifications or guaranteed commercial outcomes.

Can computational models replace testing?

No. Models can help prioritize promising formulations and experiments, but they cannot replace physical validation. Feedstock variability, equipment behaviour and real production conditions can change results. Teams developing blocks, pavers and masonry must measure performance, review application requirements and assess safety. Relevant standards and independent testing may be required before material use.

Is the platform commercially established?

The platform and its underlying data are still being built. Better Materials is focused on helping manufacturers explore waste-derived construction materials with computational intelligence. Its aim is to reduce unnecessary trial and error by considering feedstocks, production constraints and targeted experiments together. Development goals should not be read as verified customer results or guarantees.
